# Elset privacy policy

Last updated: [date]

Elset is a satellite tracker made by [seller name]. It has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking, and it has no server of its own. We do not collect any personal data.

## Your location

If you give Elset your location, or set one yourself, it is used on your device to predict satellite passes, draw the sky view and footprints, and schedule pass alerts. It is never sent to us or to any satellite-data service. It leaves your device only in two cases, both Apple services:

- Apple Maps (MapKit) receives the coordinate, or your search text, to name a place.
- If you turn on iCloud sync, your saved sites are stored in your own private iCloud database.

## iCloud sync

iCloud sync is off by default. When it is on, your watchlist, chosen categories, custom satellites, saved sites and pass-alert settings are stored in your private iCloud database, under your Apple Account. We cannot see this data. Turn sync off in the app, or manage it in your iCloud settings.

## Calendar and notifications

If you add passes to Calendar, Elset asks for write-only access and cannot read your calendar. Pass alerts are local notifications scheduled on your device. When iCloud sync is on, Apple's push service silently tells the app that another of your devices has changed something. These pushes carry none of your data.

## Data services the app contacts

To show satellites and the Earth, Elset downloads public data over HTTPS from CelesTrak, NASA, NOAA, a EUMETSAT cloud-map mirror (clouds.matteason.co.uk), sat.trackr.live, Wikidata and Wikipedia. These requests contain only public satellite identifiers, map tile coordinates or a satellite name you search for. Like any web server, these services may see your IP address, under their own privacy policies.

Links you tap, such as a Wikipedia article or a satellite operator's website, open in your browser.

## Children

Elset is suitable for all ages and collects no data from anyone.
